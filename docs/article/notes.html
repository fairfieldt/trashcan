Where the numbers come from

Back to the article

Paths are in the trashcan repository. Unless a note says otherwise, measurements are from the six-Mac chain with the G-iq4 recipe and --head-split 12 --context 32768 --batch 128 --ngram disk, every GPU at 850 MHz core / 1370 MHz memory while computing. “Calculated” means arithmetic on measured or specified values; “modeled” means a projection from a cost model. No new runs were made for this page.

  1. Hardware. Mac Pro 6,1, released December 2013 (12 years 10 months old at publication). Two AMD FirePro D700 per Mac, each 6 GiB GDDR5 on a 384-bit bus (Apple: 264 GB/s, 3.5 TFLOP/s); the two cards’ memories are separate, not one 12 GB pool. Interconnect: Thunderbolt 2. The G-iq4 weights are 116 GiB. docs/hardware.md, docs/engine-spec.md.
  2. Model. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (qwen4exp): 177B parameters (121B routed experts, ~6B used per token; a 51B hashed n-gram embedding read at layer 1; 4.6B dense). “180B” in the title is 177B rounded. 48 layers: 36 Gated DeltaNet, 12 Qwen Sparse Attention (layers 3, 7, …, 47). docs/qwen4.md.
  3. Quality. KL divergence of each candidate’s next-token distribution from the Q8_0 release over 39K tokens (19 chunks of 2,048), llama.cpp on CPU; the share of positions where both pick the same top token. Fidelity to the 8-bit model, not a capability benchmark. results/qwen4/quant-20261006.
  4. Weights and GPU count. 116 GiB total, 51 GiB of it the n-gram table at Q8_0, read through the page cache from the driver’s SSD (0.40 ms p50 per token). 65 GiB = 116 − 51 and 1.4 GiB = 65 ÷ 48 are calculated averages; the 65 GiB also includes the embedding and output head. The per-GPU split is four consecutive layers, which is three DeltaNet and one sparse-attention layer because attention is every fourth layer. Five Macs do not fit 4-bit experts, four only 2-bit ones (modeled with tools/qwen4_budget.py). docs/qwen4.md (N-gram table placement, VRAM).
  5. Links. Residual payload 4 streams × 2,560 f32 = 40 KiB per token. 25 µs round trip for 64 B over USB4STREAM, measured on a loopback cable; 9.6–9.7 Gb/s one way per cable. Ring order incite, inflight, alight, indite, invite, enlight (2026-10-14). docs/kernel.md, results/kernel/tb-loopback-20261006.
  6. Serving. trashcan serve runs requests serially through one resident engine and sequence. docs/server.md, docs/qwen4.md (Serving).
  7. Speed. Generation 22.52 / 22.54 tok/s, 128 tokens after a 512-token prompt (main 4f36bf0, 2026-10-14, Linux 7.3, USB4STREAM; that suite stopped early when invite went down). Prompt reading 1,249.3 tok/s at 30,000 tokens (main 15e5457, 2026-10-14). results/qwen4/main-suite-20261013, results/qwen4/decode-kernels-20261014.
  8. Memory and power. incite GPU 0 (layers 0–3, the n-gram module, the first twelfth of the head, 32K context, 128-token chunks): 5,830 MiB allocated, driver budget 5,869 MiB (measured); 39 MiB is the difference. K/V per position of one sparse-attention layer at f16: 2 KV heads × 256 × (K + V) × 2 bytes = 2,048 bytes (calculated; 400 MiB at 204,800 positions in docs/qwen4.md). The DeltaNet state and indexer keys would add a few MiB more per request. Power: 20.61 tok/s with six Macs at 1,357–1,364 W, from the SMC sensors over the same decode windows (2026-10-13, main 4307169); 15,150 = 20.61 ÷ 1,360.5 W × 106 (calculated). docs/qwen4.md (VRAM), results/qwen4/decode-host-20261013.
